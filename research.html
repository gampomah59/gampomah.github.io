Research

I combine synthesis, recombinant protein chemistry, biophysical analysis, and cellular assays to understand how proteins are regulated, and how that regulation changes in disease.

What can long-lived animals teach us about α-synuclein?

α-Synuclein aggregation is central to Parkinson's disease. I expressed and purified α-synuclein from eight animal species and compared their aggregation kinetics, fibril morphology, sedimentation behavior, and protease susceptibility. Sequences from long-lived species generally aggregated less than those from shorter-lived species, including humans.

How do multiple modifications reshape HSP27 function?

Post-translational modifications rarely act in isolation. Using solid-phase peptide synthesis, recombinant expression, and protein semisynthesis, I build homogeneous HSP27 proteoforms with site-specific O-GlcNAcylation and phosphorylation to study how modification crosstalk regulates its structure and chaperone activity.

Separating biological labeling from chemical artifacts

Metabolic chemical reporters let researchers visualize and enrich glycans in cells, but nonenzymatic reactions can complicate interpretation. I synthesized and validated probes for labeling O-GlcNAc-modified proteins and developed a mammalian cell assay to distinguish enzymatic incorporation from chemical S-glycosylation.
