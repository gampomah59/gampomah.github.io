About me

From natural-product chemistry to protein semisynthesis, I have followed questions that sit at the interface of between chemistry and biology.

My path into chemical biology

I began my scientific training at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Kumasi, Ghana. As an undergraduate, I worked across natural-products isolation, antimicrobial testing, small-molecule synthesis, and computational studies of ligand–protein interactions. This set me on the path to explore the roles of proteins in human disease.

At the University of Southern California, I joined Professor Matthew Pratt’s laboratory to pursue a Ph.D. in Chemistry. My graduate research integrates recombinant protein production, solid-phase peptide synthesis, protein semisynthesis, biophysical characterization, and cell-based assays. Across distinct projects, one question connects my work:

How can chemical precision reveal the ways protein sequence and post-translational modification control biological behavior?

I am especially interested in expanding this foundation toward chemical proteomics, mass spectrometry, and translational drug discovery.
